The nature of healing

Nature calms the body, and reshapes the nervous system.

Why the body settles in the natural world, the evidence, the somatic experience, and how it becomes a path.

What the research shows

The evidence that nature heals

This is real, measurable science. Across millions of participants, contact with the natural world measurably lowers stress, anxiety, and depression, and helps the body regulate.

↓ anxiety

Large, consistent effects

A second-order meta-analysis spanning 116 reviews and thousands of studies found nature-based interventions meaningfully reduce anxiety and depression and calm the body (lower heart rate).

PTSD

Helpful for trauma too

A meta-analysis of nature-based interventions found a moderate reduction in post-traumatic stress symptoms, at low cost and with few side effects.

10 min

Even a small dose

As little as ten minutes in nature yields real short-term benefit for adults with mental-health struggles. You don't need a wilderness expedition to begin.

blue

Water has its own power

Reviews of “blue space”, time in and beside water, link it to better mental health and wellbeing, over and above green space alone.

Sources (peer-reviewed): Saeedy Robat et al. (2026), Nature Human Behaviour; Genç & Yalçınkaya Alkar (2025), Journal of Environmental Psychology; Bettmann et al. (2024), Ecopsychology; Britton et al. (2020), Health Promotion International. Full references in the reading library.

Why it works

The somatic experience: healing that moves through the body

Trauma is stored below language, in the body and the survival brain. You cannot reason your way out of a flashback, and understanding your history, on its own, rarely reaches the place the fear is held. That is why healing has to be felt, not only understood.

Nature is uniquely good at reaching that place. It speaks the body's own language, through the senses, not the story:

  • Interoception: the sea, the forest, the ground under you gently draw attention back into the body, rebuilding the felt sense of being here, now, and safe.
  • The dive reflex: cold water on the face slows the heart within seconds, a built-in calming response you can learn to use on purpose.
  • Buoyancy and rhythm: being carried by water offers the nervous system a rare, wordless experience of being held.
  • Co-regulation: practised in a small circle, in a safe place, the body borrows calm from others and slowly learns that connection is safe.

None of this replaces understanding. It completes it. First the body finds steadiness; then the story can be met.

From evidence to a path

How this becomes healing, the four layers

The science and the somatic experience come together in four layers, the model that shapes everything I offer.

01

Understand the roots

Make sense of what really happened, not “what's wrong with you,” but what happened, and how it shaped your brain and body. Here a scientist's training serves you: the real science of trauma, made clear.

02

Move it through the body, in nature

Understanding isn't enough. Healing has to move, from the mind into the body, through breath, movement, nature, and above all, water.

03

Heal in relationship

The wound happened with others; the healing happens with others too. As mammals, we were never meant to heal alone. We do this held by a circle.

04

Walk it with someone who has

Not guided by theory alone, but companioned by someone who has walked this path herself, not as a therapist, but as an experienced guide who can point at what helped.
